Pairing glow: the core (opaque, slate) and the cloud (translucent) are density surfaces; the light is the pair density ξ, which lives where orbitals are fractionally occupied (it peaks near ρ ≈ 0.07 fm⁻³, in the nuclear surface). It appears above the "glow above" fraction of its maximum, coloured by its phase (hue or twilight; the phase of Δ is the same), with brightness ∝ |ξ|^γ ("glow contrast" γ, default 2: half the maximum pairing density glows at a quarter of the peak), on one scale for the whole run, so frames and regions with more pairing glow more; the core hides what is inside it, so the glow reads as a pairing skin around the core. The condensate phase turns in time at about 2λ/ħ, so the colours cycle. Coloured surfaces: the same two surfaces painted with ξ (colour strength = |ξ|, slate = unpaired). In the separate n or p surfaces the thresholds apply to that species alone. Split n | p: neutrons on the left, protons on the right, one camera; each pane draws the density of its own species with the core and cloud thresholds scaled by N/A and Z/A, so both show the same relative density level.
